Ex Nihilo

Ten interactive machines, from a ray marcher to a Lisp. Every pixel, sound and decision on this page is computed live from code written for it: no libraries, no frameworks, no images.

Enter the workshop

Move the pointer to orbit the scene

Nothing is imported.

The physics, the audio synthesis, the game engine, the programming language: each is implemented on this page in plain JavaScript and GLSL. The only outside resources are three typefaces.

Only what you see runs.

Each machine wakes when it scrolls into view and sleeps when it leaves, so ten simulations cost no more than one. Watch the frame around a bench brighten as it comes alive.

Every machine shows its source.

Every bench has a “View source” control that opens the real code running on this page, highlighted by a tokenizer that is itself part of the page. Nothing is hidden behind a build step.

Reaction, diffusion

Two virtual chemicals spread across a float texture and react, twelve times a frame, on the GPU. Change the feed and kill rates by a few thousandths and the same equation grows coral, dividing cells, or worms. Press and drag to paint chemical into the dish.

GLSLEscape-time iterationSmooth colouring

The Mandelbrot set

Each pixel iterates z ← z² + c up to four hundred times and is coloured by how quickly it escapes. Scroll to zoom toward the pointer, drag to pan, double-click to dive. In Julia mode the pointer becomes the constant c and the whole set morphs as you move. Around a hundred-thousand-fold magnification the 32-bit floats run out of digits, and the readout will tell you so.

Canvas 2DVerlet integrationConstraint relaxation

A tearable cloth

Twelve hundred points, each remembering only where it was a moment ago. Their neighbours are joined by distance constraints that are relaxed four times per step, which is what gives the fabric its weight and drape. Drag it around, or pick up the scissors and cut. Threads glow as they stretch, and snap when pushed too far.

Canvas 2DReynolds 1986Spatial hash

A flock with no leader

Three hundred and sixty birds, each obeying three local rules: keep your distance, match your neighbours’ heading, drift toward their centre. None of them knows what a flock is. A grid of buckets keeps every neighbour lookup cheap. Move the pointer into the sky and become the hawk.

Canvas 2DDiscrete Fourier transformComplex numbers

Anything, as circles

Draw a closed shape with the pointer. The machine treats your line as a list of complex numbers, takes its Fourier transform, and rebuilds it from spinning circles chained tip to tail, largest first. Keep a few terms for a ghost of the shape; keep them all and the pen traces it exactly.

Depth-first carvingA* searchBinary heap

Finding the way out

A maze is carved by a depth-first walk, then solved live. The heuristic weight changes the solver’s character: at zero it is Dijkstra, thorough and blind; at one it is textbook A*, optimal and guided; at three it is greedy, fast and occasionally wrong. Cyan is what it explored, brass is the route it found. The count of cells expanded is the honest measure of how hard each one had to think.

An opponent that looks ahead

Reversi against an engine that searches as many moves ahead as it can in under half a second, trying the most promising lines first so it can prune the rest. It weighs corners, edges and mobility, and counts discs only when the end is near. You play the dark discs; the dots are your legal moves. It will tell you how deep it looked and how many positions it considered.

A language in two hundred lines

A Scheme-flavoured Lisp: closures, recursion, first-class functions, quoting, lexical environments, and proper tail calls, so a loop of a hundred thousand iterations needs no stack. It halts runaway programs before they can freeze the page. Edit anything and run with Ctrl+Enter.

Web AudioSubtractive synthesisLook-ahead scheduler

An instrument, not a recording

There are no samples here. The pluck is two detuned oscillators through a resonant filter that sweeps on every note; the kick is a sine wave whose pitch falls from 160 to 45 Hz in a tenth of a second; the snare and hat are shaped noise. The reverb convolves against an impulse the page synthesises when you press play. Toggle steps, or ask for a new pattern built from Euclidean rhythms and a random walk on a pentatonic scale.

Made from nothing, on purpose

This page was written by Claude in a single session on 4 September 2026, when asked to build something that showed what it could do. Rather than assemble libraries, it chose to build every part in the open: the GPU shaders, the physics, the audio engine, the game engine, the search algorithms and the interpreter are all here in the source, readable from any bench.

None of the machines is a toy version. The ray marcher has soft shadows and ambient occlusion. The reaction-diffusion runs on float framebuffers. The Othello engine reaches eight or nine plies. The Lisp has proper tail calls. The synthesizer schedules ahead of the audio clock, the way real sequencers do.

Everything here is a starting point. Any bench could become a product, a lesson, or a much larger machine.
